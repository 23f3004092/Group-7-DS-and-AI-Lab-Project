IPM Package of Practices (POP) for management of Paddy blast
Paddy blast caused by a fungus Magnaporthe oryzae (formerly Magnaporthe grisea).
Paddy blast is ranked most important disease among the many diseases. It is found wherever
paddy is grown and it is always a threat.
Major states affected due to paddy blast are West Bengal, Uttar Pradesh, Punjab, Bihar,
Chhattisgarh, Odisha, Assam, Haryana, Tamil Nadu, Andhra Pradesh, Karnataka etc
Symptoms
Fungus can infect paddy at all growth stages and all aerial parts of the plant leaves, neck,
nodes and seeds. Leaf and neck infections are more severe and infection during seedling stage
may cause severe damage to the crop.
(i) Leaf blast
 Diagnostic symptoms are diamond shaped lesions on leaves with ashy centre and dark
brown margin
 Lesions may initially appear gray-green and water-soaked with a darker green border
and they expand rapidly to several centimeters in length
 They grow in size and later coalesce with other lesions to form a blighted appearance
 Severe cases entire crop gives a blast or burnt appearance
(ii) Neck blast
 The neck blast also called as neck rot or rotten neck.
 Rotten neck symptoms appear at the base of the panicle starting at the node.
 The infected tissue turns dark brown to black and shrivel causing the stem to break.
 Panicles usually develop a black colour and shrivel either completely or partially, often
they break and the heads fall to the ground.
 Neck blast cause whiteheads or white panicles, similar to stem borer infection
Lesions can be found on the panicle branches, spikes, and spikelets. The lesions are often
gray brown discolorations of the branches of the panicle and over time, the branches may break
at the lesion. Nodes become black and break up. Infected seeds consist of brown spots and
blotches.

Disease Cycle and Epidemiology
Rice blast is favored by low temperatures (22-280C), high relative humidity (>95%), dew
deposits, leaf wetness for more than 10 hours, application of high nitrogen and aerobic soils. The
disease is serious problem in upland, irrigated and hilly ecosystem. In high rainfall zones
(rainfall >1,500 mm) of north and north-eastern India, the disease is prevalent during June-
September. In Western and Central India (rainfall around 1,000 mm) the disease occurs during
August -October.
(i) Economic thresh hold level:
Leaf blast: 3-5 lesions/leaf at vegetative stage
Neck blast: 2-5 neck infected plants/m2
Management practices:
I. Nursery/seedling stage to transplanting stage:
 Keep the field clean, remove infected seeds, panicles, and plant debris after harvest.
 Crop rotation with non host crops, it provides a mechanism that separates viable
spores in crop residue from the newly emerging seedlings
 Use high quality disease free certified seeds. Infected seeds may serve as inoculum for
further spread and development.
 Treat the seeds with Pseudomonas fluorescence 10g/lit of water for 30 minutes or
Trichoderma viridae @5-10 g/kg of seeds gives protection soil borne and seed borne
diseases.
 Dip the seedlings in with Pseudomonas fluorescence 5g/lit of water for 20 minutes
before transplanting. Pseudomonas suppresses the diseases and also acts as plant
growth promotion and reduces the severity of many diseases.
 Apply neem cake @150 kg/ha
II. Vegetative to panicle initiation stage:
 Regular monitoring of disease incidence is very essential to take necessary proactive
measures
 Balanced use of fertilizers and micro-nutrients as per local recommendations. Apply
nitrogen fertilizers in three split doses 50 percent at basal dose, 25% in tillering stage
and 25% in panicle initiation stage
 Removal and destruction (burn) of diseased plant parts
 Maintaining a proper water level for the paddy to grow. Rice blast is known to be
more severe in fields or parts of fields in which the water in paddy falls below
recommended levels.
 Rice blast disease forecast: fore casting of favorable conditions for leaf blast disease
development, prediction of probable out breaks or increases in the disease intensity
allowing if when and where a particular disease management practices should be
applied.
 Foliar spray with Pseudomonas fluorescence 5g/lit of water at an interval of 15-20
days after transplanting
 Spray recommended fungicides only after the observation of initial infection of the
disease
III. Flowering to harvest stage
 Regular monitoring of disease incidence
 Foliar spray with Pseudomonas fluorescence 5g/lit of water at an interval of 15-20
days after transplanting.
 Spray recommended need based fungicides only after the observation of initial
infection of the disease.
 Spray with fungicides having lesser waiting period
